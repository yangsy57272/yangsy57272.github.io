How one thing led to another

My growth is a connected system.

I don’t experience mathematics, code, engineering, writing, and sport as separate lists. Each one changes how I approach the next problem. Follow the paths below to see how the pieces connect.

PROOF → CONSTRUCTION

Geometry became graph theory.

Olympiad-style geometry proofs led me toward graph theory and combinatorics. In the no-three-in-a-line paper, I turned a visual construction into arithmetic constraints, proved f(3,n) = min(2n, 6), and used code to enumerate configurations up to 20 columns.

ARGUMENT → COMMUNICATION

Debate taught me to make thinking legible.

Debate, speech, John Locke writing, and The New York Times sharpened my evidence, critical thinking, and communication. I carried those skills into starting a newspaper club and helping speakers clarify complex ideas as TEDx Vice President.

TRAINING → RESILIENCE

Flag football gave the week a different rhythm.

Balancing intensive training with academic commitments taught me discipline, recovery, and how to keep showing up when several demanding things matter at once.

Computer Vision · Live Device

ScentSense — turning expression recognition into scent

I led the technical build: deploying an expression-recognition model on a Raspberry Pi, connecting its output to four scent channels, and designing eight versions of the casing. When predictions fluctuated between consecutive frames and made the scent switch rapidly, I added a five-frame voting window. The final device reached 87.3% accuracy with a 0.38-second response time. A primary school later paid RMB 4,000 for it, which made operational stability feel as important as a model’s headline score.

Python EfficientNet-B0 Raspberry Pi Computer Vision 3D-printed enclosure

No three in a line

A small grid, a simple rule: choose points without putting three on one straight line. My paper studies three-row grids, translating the geometry into arithmetic conditions and using computation to enumerate configurations up to 20 columns, including their symmetries.

Energy Engineering

Greenvolt Spark — a wave-powered monitoring prototype

A typhoon warning on a Fujian beach made me wonder how offshore monitoring continues through prolonged storms. My teammate and I built a prototype that combines triboelectric and piezoelectric harvesters to charge a battery for sensors, with an ESP32 intended to transmit readings. Extending the idea toward AI raised a harder question: could a probabilistic model distinguish a real environmental change from unstable power or sensor failure?

Four problems, AIME-style.

Every answer is an integer from 0 to 999. No calculator — that's the fun of it.

i.

How many positive integers less than 1000 are divisible by 7 but not by 11?

Count the multiples of 7, then subtract the multiples of 77.
ii.

In how many ways can 8 identical debate trophies be arranged on 3 different shelves (a shelf may be empty)?

Stars and bars: 8 stars, 2 bars.
iii.

How many positive integers less than 1000 are multiples of 3 or 5?

Inclusion–exclusion: count multiples of 3, add multiples of 5, subtract multiples of 15.
iv.

Find the remainder when 22026 is divided by 1000.

Work mod 8 and mod 125 separately. Powers of 2 mod 125 repeat every 100.
