About Me

Who I am

Hi, I'm Wendy. I'm an IB student at Shanghai Pinghe, and I like ideas best before they're finished. For two years I sat in the back row on purpose so a half-formed thought could wobble on the page before anyone read it. Lately I've been practising the opposite: putting the unfinished idea on the table and seeing what happens when other people join in.

Debate taught me that first. I've argued under WSDA and TOC formats with five different partners, won a national title, and collected Outstanding Speaker awards along the way. The skill I'm proudest of is quieter than any of that: restating both sides of an argument until each person recognises their own point.

I'm happiest when a problem has a gap in it. I qualified for AIME through AMC 10A, took a Bronze at the Canadian Chemistry Olympiad, and finished in the top 4% worldwide at HiMCM. Right now I'm stuck, happily, on the no-three-in-line problem, which nobody has solved yet.

Outside class I build robots (regional champions in Istanbul in 2025 and Shanghai in 2026), design pages for the school newspaper, film school events, teach English to students from rural communities, and wander cities with a camera. My grandmother sorts plums faster than I can see. I'm slower, but I've learned to find the one that's off.

Research

Things I Built to Find Out

Four projects that started with a question nobody assigned, and got interesting the moment something broke.

01
Computer Vision

Facial Expression & Identity Recognition System (Dlib)

Independently designed and built a multimodal biometric system combining face identity verification with real-time emotion classification using Python and Dlib. The system performs facial feature extraction, identity matching, and expression recognition in a single pipeline — a novel combination not available in competing open-source tools. Includes a custom sample database, algorithm optimization across multiple test cycles, and a formal research paper documenting the methodology and results.

The fun part: I built it into an aromatherapy machine that reads your expression and releases a matching scent. At a school showcase it misread my friends' faces. So I split the model into detection, alignment, feature extraction and classification and tested each stage on its own. The culprits were a drifting bounding box and too few “angry” faces in the training data.

Python Dlib Computer Vision CTB Competition (2026) Generation AI Hackathon
02
Mathematical Modeling

Emergency Evacuation Strategy Optimization (HIMCM 2024 & 2025)

Two consecutive years of independent research for the High School Mathematical Contest in Modeling, culminating in a Finalist finish (Top 4% globally) in 2025. Research focused on multi-agent dynamic risk modeling and evacuation pathway optimization in complex building scenarios under fire and gas leak conditions. Applied Koschmieder Visibility Model, Gompertz mortality functions, and HPC-environment simulation. Produced full research papers and strategy flow diagrams.

The team part: at first each of us was solving a different problem: shortest routes, crowd density, how panic moves a bottleneck. I wrote down the assumptions we'd been leaving unspoken and asked everyone what they were really trying to solve. By library closing time it was one model, and nobody had given up their question.

Multi-agent Modeling Gompertz Functions HPC Simulation HIMCM Finalist Top 4%
03
Energy Engineering

TENG-Based Hybrid Wave Energy Harvesting Device (Conrad Challenge)

Designed and prototyped a composite wave energy collection device integrating triboelectric nanogenerators (TENG) and piezoelectric effects to convert ocean wave energy into usable electricity. Completed full SolidWorks mechanical structural design, hardware selection, 3D printing and laser-cut model assembly, partial software programming, and a commercial feasibility analysis. The project incorporated smart remote monitoring and intelligent control integration. Awarded Silver Award at the Conrad Challenge National Stage.

The moment: halfway through our pitch a judge interrupted. The physics we'd built on was his own field, and he waved the other judges over to listen.

TENG SolidWorks 3D Printing Energy Harvesting Conrad Silver Award
04
Combinatorics

The No-Three-in-Line Problem

A grid, some points, one rule that looks friendly: how many points can you place so that no three ever sit on a straight line? It's still an open problem. My paper works through it using graph theory and combinatorics, and the hardest discipline was honesty: marking the step that doesn't quite follow instead of hurrying past it to make the proof look finished.

Four problems, AIME-style.

Every answer is an integer from 0 to 999. No calculator — that's the fun of it.

i.

How many positive integers less than 1000 are divisible by 7 but not by 11?

Count the multiples of 7, then subtract the multiples of 77.
ii.

In how many ways can 8 identical debate trophies be arranged on 3 different shelves (a shelf may be empty)?

Stars and bars: 8 stars, 2 bars.
iii.

How many positive integers less than 1000 are multiples of 3 or 5?

Inclusion–exclusion: count multiples of 3, add multiples of 5, subtract multiples of 15.
iv.

Find the remainder when 22026 is divided by 1000.

Work mod 8 and mod 125 separately. Powers of 2 mod 125 repeat every 100.
