No three in a line

A grid of dots, a surprisingly short bound, and the proof behind it.

The question

How many points fit on a three-row grid if no straight line can pass through three of them? Each row can hold at most two points. Each column can hold at most two as well. Together these give the upper bound min(6, 2n) for n columns.

From drawing to proof

Numbering the columns made a diagonal through three rows easier to detect. With equally spaced rows, the middle column must be the average of the other two. I wrote Python to enumerate configurations up to twenty columns and used explicit constructions and counting to prove the bound is attainable.

Try the small case

The Playground gives you a three-by-five board to test the same constraint. A conflicting move draws the line through all three points, including diagonals that skip columns.
